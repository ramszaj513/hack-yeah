\begin{frame}{The solution: Verifact}
  \adminikicker{what it is}
  \vspace{2mm}

  \adminilead{A browser extension that checks a video's claims while you watch,
    and shows the evidence behind every verdict.}

  \vspace{3mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Reads the captions, extracts the checkable claims, and drops opinions
          and predictions.
    \item Retrieves evidence per claim from reference, academic and
          fact-checking sources.
    \item Adjudicates, then argues against itself before publishing a verdict.
    \item Fetches every cited page and confirms the quoted line is on it.
    \item Also checks any highlighted text, and reads how the speech argues---
          its nature and tone.
  \end{itemize}
  \note{%
    Keep this brief. Verifact is a browser extension: it reads the video's own
    captions (no speech-to-text), pulls out the checkable claims, gathers
    evidence from Wikipedia, the academic literature, fact-checkers and the open
    web, decides each claim, and then fetches every cited page to confirm the
    quote is really there. Beyond a video, the viewer can highlight any passage
    and check that too. A second pass reads the nature and tone of the speech:
    how it argues, beside whether its facts are right. The result is a
    decision aid, not a verdict machine.%
  }
\end{frame}
